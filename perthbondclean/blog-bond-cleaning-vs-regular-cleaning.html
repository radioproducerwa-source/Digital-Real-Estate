Bond Cleaning vs Regular Cleaning — What's the Difference?

Published May 2025  |  5 min read

A lot of Perth tenants make the same mistake: they book their usual house cleaner for their end-of-lease clean, hand back the keys, and then get a call from their property manager about a list of cleaning issues. The problem isn't that their cleaner did a bad job — it's that a regular house clean and a bond clean are fundamentally different things.

Here's exactly how they differ, and why it matters for your exit inspection.

What Is a Regular House Clean?

A regular or routine house clean is designed to maintain a home that's already reasonably clean. It typically covers:

  • Vacuuming and mopping floors
  • Wiping down benchtops and surfaces
  • Cleaning bathrooms and toilets
  • Dusting accessible surfaces
  • Cleaning the exterior of appliances

Regular cleaning is about upkeep. A cleaner working to a routine schedule has a fixed amount of time and a consistent scope — they're not deep cleaning, they're maintaining.

What Is a Bond Clean?

A bond clean — also called an end-of-lease clean or vacate clean — is a comprehensive deep clean of every surface, area, and appliance in the property, designed to restore it to the condition documented in the ingoing Property Condition Report.

It's governed by the REIWA exit condition checklist and includes areas a regular clean would never touch:

  • Oven interior — racks, cavity, door glass, and seal
  • Rangehood filters degreased
  • Inside all cupboards and drawers
  • Window tracks and weep holes
  • Blind slats — each one individually
  • Skirting boards along every wall
  • Light fittings and ceiling fans
  • Inside the dishwasher
  • Wardrobe interiors including tracks
  • Garage floor and walls

Why a Regular Cleaner May Not Be Enough

Even an excellent regular cleaner may not know what a Perth property manager specifically checks on the REIWA exit condition report. Bond cleaning is a specialist skill — it's not just about cleanliness, it's about meeting the standard set by a specific document that your property manager will be working through item by item.

A cleaner who's great at fortnightly house maintenance may not know to soak the rangehood filters, scrub the oven seal, or wipe each blind slat individually. When your bond is on the line, you want someone who knows exactly what property managers check for — because their guarantee depends on it.

Can a Regular Cleaner Do a Bond Clean?

Some can, yes — but only if they're experienced in end-of-lease cleaning specifically, work from the REIWA checklist, and offer a bond back guarantee. If your regular cleaner ticks those boxes, they may be perfectly suited. If they don't offer a guarantee, that's a meaningful signal.

The Safe Option

Booking through a dedicated bond cleaning service gives you a cleaner who does this every day, knows the REIWA checklist inside out, and backs the work with a guarantee. If anything is flagged by your property manager within 72 hours, they return and fix it free of charge.

For a 3-bedroom home, the cost difference between a regular clean and a professional bond clean is typically $150–$250. Compare that to the $300–$800 bond deductions that come from a failed exit inspection, and the maths is straightforward.
