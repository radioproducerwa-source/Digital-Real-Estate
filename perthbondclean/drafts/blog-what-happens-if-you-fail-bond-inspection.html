What Happens If You Fail Your Bond Inspection in Perth?

Published 2026  |  5 min read

Failing a bond inspection is stressful, but it's more common than most renters realise — and it doesn't automatically mean your bond is gone. In Western Australia, the process is governed by the Residential Tenancies Act 1987 and involves specific steps that both landlords and tenants must follow. Here's a clear breakdown of what happens after a failed inspection and what your options are.

Step 1: The Property Manager Issues a Breach Notice or Reinspection Request

If the exit inspection reveals cleaning issues, the property manager will typically do one of the following:

  • Issue a formal cleaning deficiency notice outlining every item that doesn't meet standard
  • Request a reinspection after you or a professional cleaner addresses the issues
  • In some cases, arrange remedial cleaning themselves and deduct the cost from your bond

The specific approach depends on the property manager and the severity of the issues. Minor issues may be resolved with a second clean; significant issues may go straight to a bond deduction claim.

Step 2: Your Response Options

After receiving a deficiency notice, you have several options:

  • Return and clean the areas yourself (if you still have access to the property)
  • Book a professional cleaner to address the specific issues
  • Dispute any items you believe were pre-existing or constitute fair wear and tear
  • Agree to a partial bond deduction to settle the matter

Acting quickly is important — delays can limit your options and allow the property manager to proceed to a formal bond deduction claim.

Step 3: Bond Deduction Claims

If the cleaning issues aren't resolved, the property manager can lodge a claim with the Bond Administrator for a deduction from your bond. They must provide evidence — typically invoices for professional cleaning — to support their claim. You have the right to dispute the amount claimed.

Step 4: Disputing the Deduction

If you disagree with the deduction amount, you can apply to the Magistrates Court (or request a hearing through the Commissioner for Consumer Protection for disputes under $10,000). To dispute successfully, you'll need:

  • Your ingoing PCR and dated photographs from move-in
  • Evidence that the issues were pre-existing (photos, written communications)
  • Arguments for fair wear and tear where applicable
  • Evidence that you completed the cleaning (receipts, photographs)

Prevention Is Better Than Disputing

The most effective strategy is to pass the inspection on the first attempt. Book a professional bond cleaner, check the REIWA standard checklist against every room, and address any outstanding issues before the inspection date. Most bond cleaning companies offer a guarantee — if the inspection fails on cleaning grounds, they'll return and reclean at no extra charge.
