How to Speed Up Your Bond Refund in WA

Published 2026  |  4 min read

Getting your bond back quickly after leaving a Perth rental depends on a combination of how well you've prepared for exit and how responsive the landlord or property manager is. While you can't force the landlord to move faster, you can take specific steps to remove every obstacle from your side and create accountability on theirs.

1. Complete a Professional Bond Clean

The single biggest cause of bond refund delays is a failed exit inspection leading to disputes about cleaning. Book a professional bond cleaner and ensure the clean is completed before the inspection date. Most professional cleaners offer a guarantee — if the property manager flags an issue, they'll return and fix it.

2. Return All Keys and Access Devices

The tenancy does not formally end until all keys, remotes, and access cards are returned. Return them in person if possible and get a written receipt. Missing keys can trigger a locksmith callout cost and delay the release of your bond.

3. Complete Your Side of the Exit PCR

Walk through the property before your exit inspection with a copy of the ingoing PCR. Note any items that have changed due to fair wear and tear and document them in writing. This creates a clear record of your position before the inspection and reduces the scope for disputes.

4. Be Present at the Exit Inspection

You are entitled to attend the exit inspection. Attending allows you to agree or dispute findings on the spot, rather than receiving a list of deficiencies days later. Issues that can be resolved in person move faster than those that require written back-and-forth.

5. Photograph the Property After the Clean

After your bond clean is complete and before you hand over the keys, photograph every room, every appliance, and every area that was included in the clean. Date-stamped photos are strong evidence if any cleaning dispute arises later.

6. Keep Your Contact Details Updated

The Bond Administrator and property manager need to reach you to finalise the refund. Ensure they have your new address, phone number, and email before you leave.

7. Follow Up if You Don't Hear Back

If you haven't received bond paperwork within 2 weeks of vacating, follow up in writing with the property manager. If they haven't responded within a further week, contact the WA Bond Administrator directly to check the status of your bond lodgement.

8. Know the Dispute Process

If the property manager is withholding your bond without a valid claim, you can apply for its release through the Commissioner for Consumer Protection. You don't need a lawyer for small bond amounts. Knowing this option exists — and communicating that you know it — can sometimes accelerate a reluctant property manager.
