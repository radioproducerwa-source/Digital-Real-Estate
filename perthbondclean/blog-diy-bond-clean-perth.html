Can I Do My Own Bond Clean in Perth? (Honest Answer)

Published May 2025  |  6 min read

It's a fair question. Bond cleans aren't cheap, you're already stressed about moving, and you've cleaned your home for years. Why pay someone else to do it? Here's the honest answer — not the one designed to sell you a cleaning service, but the one that actually helps you make the right call.

What Does WA Law Say?

The Residential Tenancies Act 1987 (WA) does not require you to hire a professional cleaner at the end of a tenancy. What it requires is that you return the property in a "reasonable state of cleanliness" consistent with the Property Condition Report completed at the start of your lease.

That means a DIY bond clean is perfectly legal — as long as the result meets the standard your property manager will be assessing against. The exception is carpet steam cleaning: many Perth leases specifically require professional steam cleaning, and if yours does, you'll need a receipt from a licensed operator.

When DIY Can Work

A self-managed bond clean can be successful if:

  • The property is small (1–2 bedrooms) and you've kept it well-maintained throughout your tenancy
  • You're meticulous and have enough time — budget 8–12 hours minimum for a 2-bedroom property
  • You work from a proper checklist (use our REIWA-aligned checklist)
  • You have or can borrow the right equipment: steam cleaner, oven degreaser, microfibre cloths, a proper mop and vacuum
  • You don't have carpet that needs professional treatment
  • Your property manager isn't particularly strict

The Real Risks of DIY

Here's where people get into trouble:

Time underestimation

A thorough bond clean of a 3x2 house takes an experienced team of 2 people 5–7 hours. Solo, it can easily take 12–16 hours. On moving day, when you also need to coordinate removalists, utilities, and keys, that time is almost impossible to find.

Missing checklist items

Property managers check everything. Blind slats (each one), the inside of the rangehood, the oven door seal, the wardrobe tracks, behind the toilet. These are the things most DIY cleaners miss — and they're exactly what gets flagged in exit reports.

No guarantee

If you do the clean yourself and the property manager finds an issue, you'll need to return to the property and re-clean it yourself. If you've already handed back the keys, this means coordinating another access time — and if the issue is significant, you may not get the chance before a claim is lodged against your bond.

The cost of failure

A failed exit inspection resulting in bond deductions typically costs $300–$800 or more. That's often more than the cost of a professional bond clean in the first place.

What Equipment Do You Actually Need?

If you do decide to DIY, here's the minimum kit for a thorough job:

  • Heavy-duty oven degreaser (oven pride or commercial-grade caustic cleaner)
  • Microfibre cloths — lots of them
  • Good vacuum with attachments for skirting boards and corners
  • Steam cleaner (hire from Bunnings ~$50/day) for tiles and grout
  • Glass cleaner and squeegee for shower screens and windows
  • Mop and bucket
  • Blind cleaning tool or soft cloths for slats
  • Step ladder for ceiling fans and light fittings
  • Toilet brush and bathroom acid cleaner for limescale

The Honest Verdict

For a small, well-maintained property and a flexible timeline, DIY can work. For anything larger than a 2-bedroom, a strict property manager, a tight timeline, or carpet that needs steam cleaning — the maths strongly favours hiring a professional.

The cost of a professional bond clean with a guarantee is $250–$700. The cost of getting it wrong is your bond, stress, and wasted time. Most Perth renters who've tried both say they'll never DIY a bond clean again.
