Oven Cleaning for Bond Inspections Perth — The Complete Guide

Published May 2025  |  5 min read

Ask any Perth property manager what they check most closely at a bond inspection, and the oven comes up every time. It's the single most common source of bond deductions — and the one area most DIY cleaners underestimate. Here's everything you need to know to get your oven inspection-ready.

Why Is the Oven So Often Flagged?

Ovens accumulate baked-on grease and carbon deposits over time, and a light wipe won't shift them. Property managers know this — they specifically check:

  • The oven cavity walls and floor
  • The roof of the oven (commonly missed)
  • Oven racks — including the rack grooves inside the cavity
  • The oven door glass — inside and outside
  • The rubber door seal around the oven cavity
  • The drawer underneath (if present) — often completely forgotten

Even if you keep the rest of the property spotless, a greasy oven will result in a cleaning deduction. In Perth, a re-clean of an oven alone typically costs $80–$150 when itemised by a property manager's contractor.

What Products Actually Work?

For Heavy Grease: Caustic Oven Cleaner

Commercial caustic-based oven cleaners (available at Bunnings and supermarkets) are the most effective option for baked-on grease. Spray liberally, leave for the recommended dwell time (usually 30–60 minutes or overnight), and wipe clean. Wear gloves — these products are strong.

For Oven Racks: Dishwasher or Soak Method

The easiest way to clean oven racks is to run them through the dishwasher on a hot cycle. If that's not possible, soak them in a bathtub with hot water and dishwashing liquid (add a cup of white vinegar for extra grease cutting) for 2–3 hours, then scrub with a scourer.

For the Door Glass: Bi-Carb Paste

Mix bicarb soda with a small amount of water to form a paste. Apply to the glass, leave for 20 minutes, then scrub with a non-scratch pad and rinse. For stubborn staining, a razor blade scraper (used carefully at a low angle) will remove baked-on deposits without scratching the glass.

For the Door Seal

Use a damp cloth with a mild cleaner. Do not use caustic products on the rubber seal — they can degrade it and you may be charged for a damaged seal rather than a dirty one.

Step-by-Step Oven Cleaning Process

  1. Remove all racks and the drawer. Set them aside to soak.
  2. Apply caustic oven cleaner to the cavity walls, floor, and roof. Follow product dwell time.
  3. While the oven soaks, clean the racks and soak the drawer.
  4. Wipe out the cavity thoroughly with damp cloths. Multiple wipe-throughs are usually needed.
  5. Clean the door glass inside with the bicarb paste method.
  6. Wipe the exterior of the oven door, control panel, and sides.
  7. Clean the seal carefully with a damp cloth.
  8. Replace the racks — they should be free of grease and carbon deposits.

Rangehood — Don't Forget This

The rangehood is checked alongside the oven and is almost as commonly flagged. Property managers look at:

  • The filters — remove and soak in hot soapy water or run through the dishwasher
  • The interior of the rangehood housing
  • The exterior and the underside

Greasy rangehood filters are impossible to miss and always noted on exit reports.

When to Call a Professional

If the oven is heavily carbonised — years of baked-on grease that has hardened — a DIY clean may not get it to the standard required. Professional bond cleaners use commercial-grade caustic products and commercial steam equipment that shift deposits a consumer product won't touch.

If your oven is more than moderately dirty, or you're short on time, handing it to a professional is the reliable choice. A full bond clean includes the oven as standard, and our cleaners are assessed specifically on oven and rangehood cleanliness before every exit inspection.
