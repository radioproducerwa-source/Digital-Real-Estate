What Drone Video Editing Involves

Drone footage editing is a specialised discipline that requires skills beyond standard video editing. Most consumer and professional drones capture footage in a flat colour profile — D-Log, D-Cinelike, or a similar log format — that looks grey and washed-out straight out of the camera. This is intentional: the flat profile preserves maximum dynamic range information for the editing stage. Realising the full potential of drone footage requires skilled colour grading that restores natural, vivid colours while retaining the wide-latitude detail that log capture provides.

Beyond colour, drone footage often requires stabilisation — even with gimbal-equipped drones, wind gusts, acceleration transitions, and proximity flying can introduce residual motion that is distracting in the final edit. We apply electronic stabilisation in post when needed, and we know how to handle the frame-crop trade-offs this involves. Shot selection and sequence pacing are also critical for drone footage: a long, uninterrupted glide over a property can be magnificent or boring depending entirely on the edit choices.

Perth Content handles all major drone footage formats: DJI (all Mavic, Air, Mini, and Inspire models), Autel, Sony, and GoPro aerial footage. We work with 4K and 6K RAW files as well as standard MP4 and MOV drone outputs.

D-Log and Colour Grading for Aerial Footage

D-Log (DJI's flat colour profile) and its equivalents from other manufacturers require a colour grading workflow before the footage is usable. Perth Content applies a professional LUT (Look-Up Table) as a starting point and then fine-tunes each clip individually, matching skies, vegetation, built surfaces, and water to produce a consistent and cinematic look throughout the edit.

For Perth's distinctive environment — bright blue sky, golden sandy beaches, terracotta rooflines, deep green native vegetation — colour grading decisions are particularly important. The challenge is preserving the sky's blue intensity without clipping to overblown highlights or letting green foliage shift toward yellow. Perth Content editors are experienced with the specific palette challenges of Western Australian aerial footage and know how to make Perth look its absolute best from the air.

Clients can request specific colour styles: clean and natural for real estate, warm and golden for hospitality and tourism, high-contrast and dramatic for architectural and infrastructure projects.

Stabilisation and Technical Cleanup

Even with the best gimbal systems, drone footage can carry micro-vibrations, wind jitter, and transition wobbles. Perth Content applies Warp Stabiliser (Premiere Pro) or similar stabilisation tools to affected clips, and knows when stabilisation helps versus when it introduces unwanted "jelly" artefacts that look worse than the original motion. Knowing when not to stabilise is as important as knowing how to stabilise.

We also handle horizon levelling (essential when the drone pans reveal a tilted horizon line), sky replacement or sky enhancement when cloud conditions are unflattering, and lens distortion correction for wide-angle drone lenses. These technical corrections are the difference between footage that looks professional and footage that looks like unedited drone video.

Combining Drone Footage With Ground-Level Video

The most impactful use of drone footage is when it is seamlessly integrated with ground-level camera work — interior shots, presenter footage, close-ups, and wide establishing shots. For real estate video Perth, the workflow is typically: aerial overview of the suburb → exterior approach → interior walkthrough → drone pull-back to show the block and street. This sequence tells a complete spatial story that neither drone footage alone nor ground footage alone can achieve.

Matching colour grades between drone and camera footage is technically challenging and is where amateur edits most often fall apart. Drone cameras and cinema cameras render colour and dynamic range very differently, and making them look cohesive in the same edit requires careful colour matching rather than simply applying the same LUT to both. Perth Content's colour pipeline handles multi-camera matching as standard.

CASA Licensing and Perth Drone Regulations

In Australia, commercial drone operations are regulated by CASA (Civil Aviation Safety Authority). Operators flying drones commercially must hold a Remote Pilot Licence (RePL) for certain aircraft categories and operations. Perth has several restricted airspace zones near Perth Airport, Jandakot Airport, and military facilities that require CASA approval before flight. Perth Content can connect you with CASA-licensed drone operators in Perth when footage capture is needed, ensuring your aerial content is captured legally and safely.
