Types of Video Perth Startups Need

Early-stage Perth startups typically need four types of video at different stages of their growth. Understanding which type serves which purpose — and what order to produce them in — helps founders allocate limited production budgets most effectively.

The pitch video (2–3 minutes) is a recorded version of your investor pitch deck. It communicates the problem, your solution, the market opportunity, the team, the traction, and the ask. Pitch videos are used in crowdfunding campaigns, sent to angel investors before meetings, embedded in applications for accelerators and grants, and shared with strategic partners. A good pitch video does not replace the live pitch — it secures the meeting for it.

The explainer video (60–90 seconds) lives on your website homepage and answers the fundamental question every new visitor has: what does this startup actually do? For technical or novel concepts, an explainer video dramatically improves conversion from visitor to lead. See our dedicated explainer video Perth page for full detail on this format. The brand film (2–4 minutes) is a more expansive story about why the startup exists, who is building it, and what success looks like. Brand films are used for hiring, partnership conversations, and investor relations at the post-seed stage.

Investor Pitch Video Tips for Perth Startups

Perth's startup ecosystem, anchored by Spacecubed, the Perth Angels network, TiE Western Australia, and the WA Government's New Industries Fund, is increasingly sophisticated. Investors who see dozens of pitch decks per month respond well to founders who communicate clearly and confidently, and a well-produced pitch video signals both preparation and professionalism.

The most common mistakes in startup pitch videos are: running too long (keep it under 3 minutes), over-explaining the technology instead of focusing on the customer problem and market size, and failing to make the team's credibility clear in the first 30 seconds. Perth Content works with startup founders on the messaging structure before any filming begins, ensuring the narrative is investor-optimised before the camera rolls.

B-roll footage of your product in use, your team working, or your customers engaging with your solution adds significant credibility to a talking-head pitch. Even simple, well-lit B-roll shot on a modern smartphone can transform a static head-and-shoulders video into a dynamic, engaging story. We advise all Perth startup clients on the minimal viable footage list for an effective pitch video.

Budget-Conscious Approaches for Perth Startups

Perth Content understands that every dollar spent on video production at the pre-revenue or early-revenue stage is a dollar not spent on product development, marketing, or team. Our startup packages are designed to deliver maximum professional impact for minimum spend through smart choices about what to produce first, how to use existing assets, and how to shoot efficiently.

Practical approaches for budget-conscious startup video: film multiple video types in a single half-day shoot to amortise location and crew costs; use the founders themselves as on-camera talent (authenticity beats polish at the pitch stage); batch your content production quarterly rather than producing one video at a time; and repurpose longer-form content (a pitch video) into shorter-form cuts (LinkedIn clips, Instagram Reels, Twitter snippets) rather than producing each format independently.

Perth Content can also work with existing recorded materials — Zoom recordings, conference presentations, demo day footage — to produce edit-only packages at reduced rates, which is often the most cost-effective path for very early-stage founders.

What Investors Look For in Startup Video

Perth angel investors and VC funds are primarily assessing three things when they watch a pitch video: the clarity and size of the problem being solved, the founding team's credibility and conviction, and the evidence of traction (customers, revenue, partnerships, or technical milestones). Everything else — production polish, graphics quality, music — is secondary to these three fundamentals.

The best startup pitch videos feel genuine. They are not over-produced to the point of seeming like corporate marketing — investors watching a pre-seed pitch expect some rawness and founder energy. What they will not accept is unclear thinking, vague market claims, or a team that cannot communicate what they do in plain language. Perth Content's startup video process always starts with getting the message right, and only then focuses on the production quality.

Perth Startup Ecosystem and Video Context

Perth's startup community has grown significantly over the past decade, with a particular strength in mining technology, agtech, health technology, and defence innovation. The city's geographic isolation, while sometimes cited as a challenge, also creates a distinctive founder culture characterised by self-reliance and resourcefulness. Perth startups raising capital often need to pitch to Sydney and Melbourne-based investors who will evaluate the team partly through video before committing to a face-to-face meeting.
